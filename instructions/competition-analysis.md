# Competition Analysis

**Origin:** MUSE Competitive Intelligence v1.0, Phases 1–2 (operator doctrine,
18 Nov 2026), plus Role 1 of the Master Skill. This file covers everything
that happens BEFORE coding starts.

## Phase 1 — Competition Intelligence

Before entering a competition:

1. Read the entire description and rules.
2. Identify the scoring metric.
3. Identify hidden-test possibilities.
4. Study inputs, outputs, constraints, and environment.
5. Check the deadline and submission requirements.
6. Analyze what kind of solution is likely to score high.
7. Identify failure risks before starting to code.

Do not choose a bounty based on prize size alone.

## Phase 2 — Strategic Selection

Analyze every opportunity:

- Technical difficulty.
- Competition requirements.
- Available tools.
- Time required.
- Potential scoring advantage.
- Submission risks.
- Estimated probability of completing successfully.

Choose the competition that best fits actual capability.

## Competition Analyst checklist (Role 1)

- Read the entire competition brief.
- Identify the exact scoring formula.
- Identify hard constraints and disqualification conditions.
- Determine how the grader evaluates submissions.
- Identify likely edge cases.
- Estimate implementation complexity.
- Build a strategy based on the actual scoring criteria.

**Never start coding before understanding how winning is measured.**

## Standing selection rules (operator)

- Submit only if plausibly top-10 after hard testing; if top-10 is not
  reachable before the deadline, do not submit — report honestly.
- Free bounties only, unless the operator explicitly approves a paid entry.
- For judged competitions: study the winners first (see the winner study in
  `competitions/makefun/`). First impression decides — adaptive pairwise
  judging gives losers ~6 matches and winners 27–107.
- For metric competitions: never submit a model/bot that loses to the
  baseline on the dev phase.
- Check the postmortems before entering a similar competition.
